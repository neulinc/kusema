## Continuous dictation

Dictate a long letter or file note without holding a key. Press
**Ctrl+Alt+Shift+D** once and keep talking: each time you pause, what you said
is typed into your document. Press it again to stop. Coming from Dragon? One
click under **Continuous Dictation** on the General page makes it **Numpad +**.

- **Voice commands:** say "new paragraph", "new line", "scratch that" or "stop
  dictation" on its own, between pauses. The same words inside a sentence are
  typed as usual. You can turn commands off.
- **It stays in your document.** If you switch to another window, typing pauses
  and the overlay says "Paused, window changed" until you come back. What you
  say while you're away is saved in History, not typed.
- **Nothing is lost.** Each piece is saved to a single History entry as you go,
  so a crash keeps everything said so far. The audio isn't kept.
- **Never left on by accident.** The overlay shows "Dictating", the time and a
  Stop button for the whole session, even if you've hidden the overlay for other
  dictation. Dictation also stops by itself after 3 minutes of silence, which you
  can change along with the length of pause that types your words.
- **Stays on your computer.** Speech recognition is local, and AI cleanup and
  cloud services are never used in this mode. Dictated text stays out of
  Windows' clipboard history and cloud clipboard too.

While you're dictating, Escape stops dictation instead of reaching the app you're
in, and other Kusema shortcuts do nothing until you stop. Windows only for now.

## Privacy page and Privileged mode

See exactly what leaves your computer, and stop it with one switch.

- **The Privacy page** lists every request Kusema makes: when, where, why, what
  kind of data and how large, but never what was in it. Audio sent off your
  computer: 0 bytes, because speech recognition runs locally. Keep the list for
  as long as you like (30 days to start), clear it, or export it as a CSV.
- **Privileged mode** guarantees nothing goes to a cloud AI service. Turn it on
  from the Privacy page, the Assistant page, the tray menu or an optional
  shortcut. Ask Kusema, Ask About My Screen and AI cleanup keep working with a
  model on your computer, such as Ollama. With a cloud provider they tell you why
  they didn't send anything, and dictation still types your words.
- **Local network AI servers** and **offline offices** each have their own
  option: allow a server on your office network, or also stop downloads and
  update checks.
- **You can see when it's on:** a "Privileged" chip in the overlay and on the
  Assistant page, a badge on the tray icon, and a note in its tooltip.

Kusema sends no telemetry or analytics. Dictated text now also stays out of
Windows' clipboard history and cloud clipboard in normal dictation.
